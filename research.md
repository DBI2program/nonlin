<h2>About NONLIN</h2>
    <p>
    NONLIN investigates how interactions across distributed brain areas give rise to cognition. While communication in the brain is often studied through linear relationships between neural signals, these approaches may capture only part of how information is coordinated and integrated across large-scale brain networks.
    </p>
    <p>
    The project explores the hypothesis that non-linear and nonseparable interactions are fundamental to large-scale brain function. It develops new mathematical methods to identify and characterise these interactions and applies them to large-scale intracranial electrophysiological recordings from humans and non-human primates.
    </p>
    <p>
    By studying how these interactions change across brain states and during cognitive processes such as attention, working memory and perceptual binding, NONLIN aims to provide a new understanding of how distributed neural activity is integrated to support cognition.
    </p>
<h2>Beyond linear interactions</h2>
    <p>
    Much of our current understanding of communication between brain areas relies on linear relationships between neural signals, including measures such as coherence and Granger causality. Yet cortical activity is highly heterogeneous: oscillations vary in frequency and amplitude across brain areas, while rapid changes in cognitive state are often associated with transient, aperiodic activity.
    </p>
    <p>
    NONLIN investigates whether large-scale cortical coordination therefore depends on interactions that cannot be adequately captured by conventional linear measures. By moving beyond a purely linear description of brain communication, the project aims to reveal additional principles underlying coordination across distributed cortical networks.
    </p>
    <h2>Non-linear &amp; nonseparable integration</h2>
    <p>
    NONLIN develops new mathematical methods to identify and characterise non-linear interactions and distinguish them from linear correlations produced by simple information relaying.
    </p>
    <p>
    A central focus is nonseparable information integration: interactions in which the combined influences of multiple brain regions on a target area cannot be decomposed into independent effects. Studying these interactions will allow NONLIN to test how information is integrated across distributed networks and challenge traditional modular descriptions of cognitive processing.
    </p>

    <h2>From brain states to cognition</h2>
    <p>
    NONLIN applies these new methods to large-scale intracranial electrophysiological recordings from humans and non-human primates. The project will examine how non-linear interactions relate to whole-brain anatomical connectivity and whether they exhibit properties distinct from the linear functional connectome.
    </p>
    <p>
    It will also investigate how non-linear and nonseparable integration changes across brain states and cognitive processes, including attention, working memory and perceptual binding, with particular attention to transient broad-band influences and cross-frequency interactions.
    </p>
